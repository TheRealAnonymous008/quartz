* A [[Fundamental Constructs of Graph Theory|graph]] $X$ is **vertex transitive** if its [[Graph Automorphism|automorphism group]] [[Group Action|acts]] [[Transitive Group|transitively]] on $V(X)$. 
* Every vertex transitive graph is [[Families of Graphs|regular]].
* (*Godsil 3.3.3*) If $X$ is a connected vertex-transitive graph, then $\kappa(X)$ is equal to its degree.
* (*Godsil 3.4.2*) A vertex transitive graph $X$ with degree $k$ has vertex [[Graph Connectivity|connectivity]]. 
	$$
	\kappa(X) \ge \frac 23 (k+1)
	$$
* (*Godsil 3.5.1*) Let $X$ be a connected vertex-transitive graph. Then $X$ has a [[Graph Matching|matching]] that leaves at most one vertex unmatched and each edge is contained in a maximum matching
	* If $X$ is vertex transitive and one vertex is critical, then all vertices are critical.
	* If, in addition to the above, $X$ is [[Edge Transitive Graph|edge transitive]], then no vertex is unmatched. 

* (*Godsil 3.6.3*) A connected vertex transitive graph on $n$ vertices contains a cycle of length at least $\sqrt{3n}$. 

* (*Godsil 3.9.1*) Any connected vertex transitive graph is a [[Graph Homomorphism|retract]] of a [[Cayley Digraph|Cayley graph]]. 
	* Let $X$ be a vertex transitive graph and  $x\in V(X)$. Let $C$ be the set 
	  $$
	  C = \set{g\in G \mid x \text{ adjacent to } gx}
	  $$
	  $C$ is a union of right [[Cosets, Group Indices|cosets]] of $G_x$ and since $x$ is not adjacent with itself $C\cap G_x =\emptyset$. Also
	  $$
	  ax \leftrightarrow bx \iff x\leftrightarrow a^{-1}bx \iff a^{-1}b \in C
	  $$
	  If $g\in C$ and $h,h'\in G_x$ then 
	  $$
	  x= hx\leftrightarrow hgx= hgh'x\implies hgh'\in C
	  $$
	  This implies $G_xC G_x \subseteq C$. In combination with the fact that $C\subseteq G_x C G_x$, we have $C=G_x C G_x$.
	  
	  We can show inductively (via the diameter of $X$) that the automorphism subgroup generated by the elements of $C$ acts transitively on $V(X)$.
	  
	  The Cayley graph we are interested in is $Y=\text{Cay}(G,C)$.  It can be shown that the right cosets of $G_x$ have no corresponding edges between them or are completely joined. The subgraph of $Y$ induced by each right coset is empty since $e\notin C$. 
	  
	  The subgraph of $Y$ induced by any complete set of coset representatives of $G_x$ is isomorphic to $X$. 
	  
	  The homomorphism we are interested in is the map, from the vertices corresponding to the cosets of $G_x$, of the vertices from $Y$ to $X$.  
	* $Y$ can be obtained from $X$ by replacing each vertex in $X$ with an independent set of size $|G_x|$.  The graph induced by a pair of them is empty when the vertices in $X$ are not adjacent or a complete bipartite subgraph if they are.
	* 
	  $$
	  \frac{|V(X)|}{\alpha(X)} = \frac{|V(Y)|}{\alpha(Y)}
	  $$
	  
* **Lovasz Conjecture**: Every vertex transitive graph has a Hamiltonian path. Every vertex transitive graph contains a [[Hamiltonian Graph|Hamiltonian cycle]] except for five counterexamples.
	* $K_2$
	* $J(5,2)$ - the [[Generalized Johnson Graph|Petersen graph]]
	* The Coxeter graph
	* The Petersen graph with vertices replaced by triangles
	* The Coxeter graph with vertices replaced by triangles.
